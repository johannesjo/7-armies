Lanes: one commander a side. Waves of foot march down all three lanes every 25 seconds. Charge enemy battalions in the flank (×2) or rear (×3) to break them, and help your own foot pull down the enemy towers — outer, then inner, then the keep. Towers shoot your foot first, but they turn on you if you ride in alone or attack their commander under the walls: dodge the red circles. Ride home into the ring round your keep to heal and to fill your squadron back up. Every enemy that falls near you gives experience; each level, choose one of three cards (keys 1–3). Knights break foot by charging its flank or rear; horse archers by circling at bowshot — their arrows shake morale, but their charge barely dents a battalion. Each kind of horse has one ability (Q or the round button): Knights' Thunder Charge (4 s faster, trampling twice as hard), Horse Archers' Arrow Storm (every rider looses three arrows at once). You only see what your foot, your squadron and your towers see — and only a little way into the woods. Lose all your riders and you're out until you can ride again. Break their keep to win.

Front: one great battle, and a commander a side. Both armies stand in one long line; every 30 seconds each sends a column of fresh foot from its rear edge to whichever sector of the line is weakest (the three bars under the level show who is winning the left, centre and right). Charge enemy battalions in the flank (×2) or rear (×3) to break them, and help wherever our line is buckling — or catch their columns on the march, before they reach the line. Pikes facing you will stop a charge. Every battalion that breaks and every man that falls wears down his army's morale; slay the rival commander to shake his whole army. An army breaks when four in five of all the foot it has fielded are dead or fled — and after nine minutes both armies tire, so every battle ends. Ride home into the ring behind your army to heal and to fill your squadron back up. Every enemy that falls near you gives experience; each level, choose one of three cards (keys 1–3). Knights break foot by charging its flank or rear; horse archers by circling at bowshot — their arrows shake morale, but their charge barely dents a battalion. Each kind of horse has one ability (Q or the round button). You only see what your troops and your squadron see. Lose all your riders and you're out until you can ride again.

Start a stroke at the end of your route to extend it. Charge into the flanks (×2) and rear (×3) of enemy battalions to break their morale. Wheel wide: on a sharp turn the riders on the outside of your wedge fall behind, and a loose wedge loses to a tight one. Hit enemy horsemen in the flank or rear to scatter them — or lead pursuing knights onto our pikes and archers. Pikes facing you will stop a charge, and enemy knights will hunt you. Keep moving — riders that stall in the crowd get pulled down. Ride into allied cavalry troops to take them under your command (up to 45 riders, a full wedge); the two blue camps behind our wings are pit stops — ride through one and its waiting riders fall in behind you, and your wounded are healed at once. The longer you stay away, the more riders gather there. A full gallop hits hardest but turns wide; slow down to turn tight.
